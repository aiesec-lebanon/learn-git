<Beat id="stage" title="The stage">
  Every lesson draws a repository as you work. The **Repository** area is the
  commit graph: each dot is a commit, the paper labels are branches, and the red
  HEAD label marks where you are. Below it, the **Working tree** holds your
  files as they are on disk, and the **Staging area** holds the draft of the
  next commit.
</Beat>

<Beat id="scrub" title="Scroll and scrub">
  In a lesson the stage stays pinned while the text scrolls. Each section runs
  its commands on the stage as you reach it, and Replay plays it again. Nothing
  you scroll past changes your own sandbox.
</Beat>

<Beat id="drag" title="Drag or tap">
  Labels and HEAD move by hand. Drag HEAD onto the `dark-mode` label, or drop
  a new label on a commit, and read the command the move stands for. On a
  phone, tap the item, then tap where it goes. With a keyboard, Space picks it
  up, the arrows choose a target and Enter drops it. Here nothing runs: the
  box below the graph only shows the command.

  <Widget name="pointer-preview" />
</Beat>

<Beat id="terminal" title="Terminal and chips">
  Each lesson ends with Your turn: a real sandbox with a terminal. Type Git
  commands as you would on your own computer. On a phone, the chips under the
  terminal build the same commands without a keyboard.
</Beat>

<Beat id="rewind" title="Rewind">
  Made a mess? Rewind steps your sandbox back one command at a time, so trying
  things costs nothing.
</Beat>

<Beat id="guide" title="The guide">
  Nib, the guide, says what each command just did in plain words and offers
  hints when you are stuck. Git's own messages stay exactly as Git prints them.
</Beat>

<Beat id="settings" title="Settings">
  Settings choose the theme, how much Nib says, and motion: with reduced motion,
  lessons move in steps and the stage shows short before and after notes instead
  of animations.
</Beat>
