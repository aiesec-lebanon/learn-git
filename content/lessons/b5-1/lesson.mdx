<ColdOpen>
  Sam keeps the bake sale site on the Hub, and you are joining him. Copying it
  takes one command. But once there are two repositories, how does yours know
  what happened in the other one?
</ColdOpen>

<Beat id="clone" title="Two complete repositories">
  `git clone` copies every commit from the Hub into a new folder. Your
  repository gets its own `main`, plus a hollow `origin/main` label: the
  <Term>remote-tracking branch</Term> that remembers where origin's main was.
</Beat>

<Beat id="origin" title="Meet origin">
  `origin` is the short name the clone gave the Hub address; `git remote -v`
  shows it. `git branch -vv` shows that your main tracks `origin/main`: that is
  its <Term>upstream</Term>, the branch it compares itself with.
</Beat>

<Beat id="last-contact" title="A bookmark, not a live view">
  Sam pushes a commit, and origin's main moves on the Hub. Your `origin/main`
  stays where it was, and `git status` still says "up to date". It compares
  against the bookmark from your last contact with origin, not the Hub itself.
</Beat>

<Beat id="all" title="Every label you have">
  `git branch -a` lists your branches and the remote-tracking ones together.
  Origin also has a `docs` branch, but you only hold `origin/docs`, a bookmark
  you can look at, not a branch of your own.
</Beat>

<Beat id="docs" title="Your own docs">
  `git switch docs` finds `origin/docs` and creates a local `docs` that tracks
  it, then switches to it. `git branch -vv` now shows both of your branches,
  each with its upstream.
</Beat>
